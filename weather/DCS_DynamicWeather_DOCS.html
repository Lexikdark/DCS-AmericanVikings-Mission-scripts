DCS World Weather System DCS 2.7+ Vulkan

Complete reference for the Mission Editor weather tab, dynamic weather behaviour, Lua mission table structure, cloud presets, and the scripting API — written for mission designers and script authors.

Overview

DCS World simulates real-world atmospheric conditions including wind at multiple altitudes, temperature, barometric pressure, cloud cover, fog, precipitation, and turbulence. These conditions affect every aspect of flight — from altimeter calibration and fuel burn to weapons delivery accuracy and AI behaviour.

Weather is configured per-mission in the Mission Editor (ME) under the Weather tab. It can be set to remain static for the entire mission, or set to dynamic where conditions gradually evolve over time.

Since DCS 2.7 (Vulkan renderer), clouds are rendered volumetrically and selected using named cloud presets rather than manual density/thickness sliders. This change significantly improved visual fidelity and made cloud definitions more consistent.

Key concepts All altitude values in DCS weather are in metres above sea level (MSL) unless otherwise noted. Wind speed is in metres per second (m/s). Wind direction follows the meteorological convention — it describes the direction the wind comes from, not the direction it blows toward.

Static vs Dynamic Weather

Static Weather

All weather parameters are fixed from mission start to finish. Wind speed, direction, pressure, temperature, and cloud cover remain exactly as configured in the Mission Editor.

Use static weather when you need a controlled, predictable environment — training missions, competitions, or scenarios where weather should not interfere.

Lua value: atmosphere_type = 0

Dynamic Weather

Weather parameters gradually shift throughout the mission, simulating natural atmospheric drift. Wind direction rotates, wind speed fluctuates, and barometric pressure rises or falls.

The initial settings you configure in the ME act as the starting point. Over the mission, conditions evolve in a realistic, continuous transition.

Lua value: atmosphere_type = 1

Dynamic weather and cloud presets In dynamic mode, volumetric clouds themselves do not visibly morph or change preset during flight. The primary dynamic elements are wind vector, pressure, and turbulence. Cloud and fog visibility may also shift subtly over time.

Wind Layers

DCS models wind at three altitude layers. Within each layer you set an independent wind speed and direction. Between layers, DCS interpolates smoothly, so wind conditions transition gradually with altitude.

Ground (0 m) Surface wind directly affects takeoff and landing performance, crosswind components, and ground unit dust effects. Maximum ~50 m/s.
2 000 m Mid-level wind layer. Represents conditions typical for tactical flight altitudes and rotary wing operations. Interpolated between Ground and 8 000 m.
8 000 m Upper layer representing high-altitude jet-stream level winds. Affects high-altitude navigation, long-range weapons drift (JDAM, cruise missiles), and high-speed aircraft cruise flight.

Wind Parameters

ParameterRangeDescription
speed0 – 50 m/sWind velocity. 0 = calm. Beaufort reference: 10 m/s ≈ Force 5 (fresh breeze); 20 m/s ≈ Force 8 (gale).
dir0 – 360°Direction wind comes from. 0/360 = North, 90 = East, 180 = South, 270 = West. Same convention as METAR wind field.
Wind direction convention A wind of dir = 270 and speed = 10 means a westerly wind — it blows from the west. Aircraft heading into this wind fly eastward into a headwind. This matches real-world aviation METAR reporting.

Crosswind Calculations

To create a crosswind scenario for a specific runway, set the wind direction roughly 90° from the runway heading. For example, with RWY 09 (090° heading), set dir = 000 or dir = 180 for a full crosswind.

Crosswind component = Wind Speed × sin(angle between wind and runway heading). Headwind component = Wind Speed × cos(angle).

Cloud Presets (DCS 2.7+ Vulkan)

Since DCS 2.7, clouds are rendered using a volumetric ray-marching system on the Vulkan graphics backend. Instead of manually setting cloud density and thickness (legacy system), you select a named preset that defines the cloud type, layer count, and visual style.

Each preset represents a real-world cloud scenario. The preset name is stored in the mission's Lua table as clouds.preset. You also independently set the cloud base altitude in metres.

Cloud Base Altitude

The clouds.base value (in metres) sets the floor of the cloud layer. Valid range is approximately 300 m – 5 000 m though the ME constrains this. The preset determines the shape and thickness above that base.

Example: base = 1500 places cloud bottoms at 1 500 m MSL. Pilots flying below this altitude will be VMC; above it they enter IMC inside the cloud layer.

Fog & Visibility

Horizontal Visibility

The visibility.distance parameter sets overall horizontal visibility in metres — how far objects, terrain, and other aircraft can be seen. It simulates haze, dust, smog, or general atmospheric clarity.

Value (m)ConditionPilot Impact
80 000 +Exceptional CAVOKFull visual range, no haze
20 000 – 79 999Good VFRNormal VFR ops, landmarks visible at range
8 000 – 19 999Reduced visibilityHazy; longer-range visual ID harder
3 000 – 7 999Marginal VFR / IFRInstrument approaches required
< 3 000Very poorNear-ground operations only, very limited targeting

Fog Layer

Fog is a separate, distinct low-altitude moisture effect layered over the terrain, controlled independently from general visibility.

ParameterDescription
enable_fogBoolean. true activates the fog layer.
fog.thicknessVertical depth of the fog layer in metres. E.g. 100 = shallow ground fog; 500 = thick fogbank.
fog.visibilityHorizontal visibility inside the fog layer, in metres. Lower = denser fog (e.g. 200 = very dense; 2000 = thin).
Fog vs Visibility — key difference visibility.distance sets general atmospheric haze across all altitudes. The fog block is a separate ground-hugging layer that only affects altitudes below fog.thickness. Both can be active simultaneously — fog creates near-zero visibility at low altitude while general haze limits longer-range viewing above the fog deck.

Precipitation

Precipitation is controlled via clouds.iprecptns in the legacy system, or implied by selecting a Rainy cloud preset in Vulkan.

ValueEffect
0 — NoneNo precipitation.
1 — RainRain particle effects, wet runway visual, slightly reduced visibility. Minimal flight model change for most fixed-wing aircraft.
2 — ThunderstormHeavy rain, lightning flashes, increased turbulence effects. Most realistic severe weather scenario.
Precipitation and cloud presets When using the Vulkan cloud system, select a RainyPreset to enable precipitation visuals automatically. Setting iprecptns = 1 without a matching rainy cloud preset may produce inconsistent visual results.

Barometric Pressure & Temperature

QNH (Sea Level Pressure)

The qnh field sets the barometric pressure at sea level in millimetres of mercury (mmHg). This directly affects:

  • Aircraft altimeter calibration — pilots must set QNH in the altimeter subscale for accurate altitude readings
  • Air density — higher pressure = denser air = more thrust and lift
  • True vs indicated airspeed relationships
mmHghPa (approx)Condition
7601 013Standard atmosphere (ISA) — most common default
720 – 749960 – 999Low pressure, stormy weather system
750 – 7691 000 – 1 025Normal range, slight gradient
770 – 7901 027 – 1 053High pressure, stable clear weather
Unit conversion 1 mmHg ≈ 1.333 hPa. To convert: hPa = mmHg × 1.333. Standard ISA = 760 mmHg = 1 013.25 hPa.

Temperature

Temperature is set in season.temperature in degrees Celsius. It affects:

  • Density altitude — higher temperatures reduce air density, increasing takeoff roll and degrading engine thrust
  • Speed of sound — affects Mach calculations and indicated vs true airspeed
  • Terrain appearance — snow/grass coverage on some maps changes with temperature setting
Range (°C)Scenario
−30 to −10Arctic / winter operations. High-altitude winter. Significant icing risk on unprotected surfaces.
−10 to 5Cold climate, winter missions. Snow likely on terrain.
6 to 20Temperate, spring/autumn. Near ISA standard.
21 to 35Warm/summer. Middle East standard. Density altitude effect starts to matter.
35+Desert hot. Significant density altitude degradation for heavily-loaded aircraft.

Turbulence

Turbulence adds random perturbations to the aircraft's flight path, simulating atmospheric bumps. Configured via groundTurbulence (and legacy turbulence_power).

ValueIntensityEffect on Aircraft
0NonePerfectly smooth air.
1 – 10LightSlight bumps. Minimal impact on weapons delivery or hover.
11 – 25ModerateNoticeable buffeting. Increased workload; affects helicopter stability.
26 – 45SevereSignificant jolts. CCIP/CCRP accuracy degrades. Hover very difficult.
46 – 60ExtremeViolent buffeting. Weapons highly inaccurate. Use only for extreme scenarios.
Turbulence and weapons delivery High turbulence values noticeably reduce CCIP dumb-bomb accuracy and laser-spot stability from rotary-wing platforms. Keep turbulence below 15 for competitive multiplayer unless intentionally simulating severe conditions.

How Dynamic Weather Works

When atmosphere_type = 1, DCS runs an internal atmospheric simulation that gradually shifts weather parameters over mission time. This section explains what actually changes, at what rate, and what you can control.

What Changes Over Time

  • Wind Direction — Gradually rotates over hours of mission time, simulating a pressure system passing through. The rotation is smooth and continuous, typically shifting tens of degrees over a full mission cycle.
  • Wind Speed — Increases and decreases in slow oscillations around the initial configured value. Large sudden gusts do not occur; changes are gradual.
  • Barometric Pressure (QNH) — Drifts up or down from the starting value, simulating a weather front moving through. Pressure changes affect altimeter accuracy over long missions without re-setting the subscale.
  • Turbulence — Fluctuates around the baseline value. The atmosphere becomes bumpier and smoother in irregular cycles.
  • Visibility / Fog density — May drift slightly, though this is less pronounced than wind and pressure changes.

What Does NOT Change Dynamically

  • Volumetric cloud preset — The cloud type does not switch visually mid-mission
  • Temperature — Fixed at the ME-configured value for the entire mission
  • Season — Fixed per mission

Rate of Change

DCS does not provide a user-facing "rate of change" slider in the Mission Editor. The internal simulation rate means winds typically shift by several degrees of direction and 1–3 m/s speed over 30–60 minutes of mission time.

The initial configured values act as the weather's starting point. To control what kind of weather a dynamic mission begins in, configure the ME settings exactly as you would for static weather.

Design tip — long persistent server missions On servers running continuous missions (8–24 hours), dynamic weather creates genuinely varied conditions across different sessions. Pilots flying at different times experience different wind components and pressure, making navigation and fuel planning more complex and rewarding.

Mission Time vs Real Time

Weather change is driven by mission time, not wall-clock time. Using DCS time acceleration (Z key) also accelerates weather changes. A mission running at 4× speed progresses weather 4× faster than real time.

Mission Lua Table Structure

The weather configuration is stored inside mission.lua (found within the .miz archive, which is a renamed ZIP file). Below is a fully annotated example showing every weather-related field.

Editing mission.lua directly Rename .miz to .zip, extract, edit mission.lua in a text editor, re-zip, then rename back. Keep a backup. For most changes the Mission Editor is safer and faster. 
-- ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
--  WEATHER BLOCK inside mission.lua
-- ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
["weather"] = {

    -- Atmosphere mode:  0 = Static   |   1 = Dynamic
    ["atmosphere_type"] = 1,

    -- ── Wind at three altitude layers ──────────────────────────────
    ["wind"] = {

        -- Upper layer  (8 000 m MSL)
        ["at8000"] = {
            ["speed"] = 8,       -- m/s
            ["dir"]   = 220,     -- degrees wind comes FROM
        },

        -- Mid layer  (2 000 m MSL)
        ["at2000"] = {
            ["speed"] = 5,
            ["dir"]   = 200,
        },

        -- Ground layer (surface)
        ["atGround"] = {
            ["speed"] = 3,
            ["dir"]   = 180,
        },
    },

    -- ── Season / temperature ────────────────────────────────────────
    ["season"] = {
        ["temperature"] = 22,    -- degrees Celsius
    },

    -- ── Barometric pressure ─────────────────────────────────────────
    ["qnh"] = 760,               -- mmHg  (760 = ISA standard = 1013 hPa)

    -- ── Cloud configuration (DCS 2.7+ Vulkan system) ───────────────
    ["clouds"] = {

        -- Volumetric preset name (string)
        ["preset"]    = "Preset4",

        -- Cloud base altitude in metres
        ["base"]      = 1800,

        -- ── Legacy fields (pre-2.7, kept for backwards compat.) ───
        ["density"]   = 5,       -- 0 (clear) to 10 (overcast)
        ["thickness"] = 400,     -- cloud layer thickness in metres
        ["iprecptns"] = 0,       -- 0=none  1=rain  2=thunderstorm
    },

    -- ── Fog ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
    ["enable_fog"] = false,
    ["fog"] = {
        ["thickness"]  = 0,      -- vertical depth of fog (m)
        ["visibility"] = 6000,   -- horiz. visibility inside fog (m)
    },

    -- ── General horizontal visibility ───────────────────────────────
    ["visibility"] = {
        ["distance"] = 80000,    -- metres  (80 km = excellent)
    },

    -- ── Turbulence ──────────────────────────────────────────────────
    ["groundTurbulence"] = 5,    -- 0 (calm) to 60 (extreme)

    -- ── Legacy turbulence parameters ────────────────────────────────
    ["turbulence_power"]     = 0,
    ["turbulence_threshold"] = 10,

},

Scripting API (SSE / Mission Scripts)

DCS's Scripting Engine (SSE) exposes weather data at runtime via the atmosphere library. These functions can be called from mission trigger DO SCRIPT actions or loaded Lua files.

atmosphere.getWind(point)

Returns the wind vector at a 3D world position as a Vec3 {x, y, z}.

-- point is a Vec3 world position {x, y, z}
-- y is altitude in metres
local windVec = atmosphere.getWind({ x = 0, y = 500, z = 0 })

-- Convert to speed (m/s) and FROM direction (degrees)
local speed = math.sqrt(windVec.x^2 + windVec.z^2)
local dir   = math.deg(math.atan2(-windVec.x, -windVec.z)) % 360
-- 'dir' = direction wind comes FROM (meteorological convention)

atmosphere.getWindWithTurbulence(point)

Same as getWind() but includes turbulence perturbations in the returned vector. Useful for realistic in-flight AI simulation.

local turbulentWind = atmosphere.getWindWithTurbulence({ x = 0, y = 1000, z = 0 })

atmosphere.getTemperatureAndPressure(point)

Returns temperature (°C) and pressure at a given point. Pressure is in Pascals (Pa).

local temp, pressure = atmosphere.getTemperatureAndPressure({ x = 0, y = 0, z = 0 })
-- temp     = degrees Celsius at that point
-- pressure = Pascals

-- Convert pressure to common units:
local qnh_hPa  = pressure / 100         -- hPa / millibars
local qnh_inHg = pressure / 3386.39     -- inches of mercury
local qnh_mmHg = pressure / 133.322     -- mmHg (DCS native unit)
No direct weather table access at runtime The mission.weather table is not directly accessible to scripts running inside the simulation. Use atmosphere.* functions to query live conditions — especially important in dynamic weather mode where values change over time.

Example — In-Game ATIS Wind Report

-- Broadcast wind conditions to all players every 5 minutes
local function broadcastATIS()
    -- Use your airfield's world coordinates as the reference point
    local pos  = { x = 0, y = 0, z = 0 }
    local wind = atmosphere.getWind(pos)

    local speedMs  = math.sqrt(wind.x^2 + wind.z^2)
    local speedKts = speedMs * 1.94384
    local dir      = math.deg(math.atan2(-wind.x, -wind.z)) % 360

    local _, pressure = atmosphere.getTemperatureAndPressure(pos)
    local qnhHPa = pressure / 100

    local msg = string.format(
        "ATIS: Wind %03d at %.0f kts | QNH %.0f hPa",
        math.floor(dir + 0.5), speedKts, qnhHPa
    )
    trigger.action.outText(msg, 15)

    return timer.getTime() + 300   -- reschedule in 5 minutes
end

timer.scheduleFunction(broadcastATIS, nil, timer.getTime() + 10)

Practical Tips for Mission Designers

Altimeter Setting (QNH)

Always include the QNH in pilot briefing materials or an ATIS script. Standard ISA is 760 mmHg (1013 hPa). Western modules (F/A-18C, F-16C, etc.) typically display QNH in hPa or inHg; Russian modules (Su-27, Ka-50) use mmHg. A wrong subscale setting causes altitude errors — dangerous for low-level operations and approach planning.

Crosswind Runway Design

To create a realistic crosswind scenario, set ground wind direction 60–90° off the primary runway heading at 8–15 m/s (16–30 kts) for a demanding but manageable crosswind for most tactical jets. Above 18 m/s exceeds the limits for many airframes. Check your module's operating limits before designing extreme wind scenarios.

Weapons Delivery in Wind

Strong upper-layer winds (at 8 000 m) directly affect unguided ballistic weapons — dumb bombs, cluster munitions, and unguided rockets drift with the wind. For CAS scenarios, setting a 15–20 m/s upper wind at varying angles makes CCIP/CCRP delivery genuinely challenging.

GPS-guided weapons (JDAMs) compensate for wind automatically during fall. Laser-guided weapons are minimally affected. Unguided rockets are most sensitive to drift at high delivery altitudes.

Fog for CSAR / Special Operations

A fog layer with thickness = 150 m and visibility = 500 m creates a realistic fogbank scenario where helicopters fly NOE below the fog deck to maintain visual, while aircraft above remain VMC. Combine with an overcast preset at 800 m base for a maximum-immersion CSAR environment.

Dynamic Weather on Persistent Servers

For long-running multiplayer servers, dynamic weather creates natural variety without scripting. Set starting conditions to a reasonable baseline — 10–12 m/s wind at 2 000 m, 760 mmHg, Preset4 clouds. Over 4–8 hours the weather will naturally shift, giving morning and evening players different conditions.

Note: Weather state is NOT saved between server restarts. Each map cycle begins fresh from the ME-configured starting values, then begins evolving again from that baseline.

Temperature and Density Altitude

For Hot & High scenarios (Middle East, summer), set temperature to 35–40°C. This creates genuine density altitude problems: helicopter maximum hover ceiling drops, jet takeoff roll increases, and afterburner thrust is reduced. Particularly impactful for heavily loaded aircraft departing from elevated airbases such as mountain airfields on the Syria map.

Thunderstorm Warning

The RainyPreset3 thunderstorm combination with groundTurbulence = 40+ creates extremely challenging conditions. AI aircraft may struggle to maintain formation, and helicopter AI may abort tasks in severe turbulence. Test thoroughly before deploying on a live server.
